# What makes a good issue?

Every issue in this project should answer three questions. If it can't, it's not ready to work on.

---

### Title
_Short, action-oriented. "Add X" or "Decide Y", not "X"._

### Description
_One or two sentences: what and why. Skip the how — that's for the PR._

### Upstream / Downstream contract
_What do you need from someone else before you can start? What will someone else need from you when you're done? Name the teammate and the interface._

### Definition of Done
_A checklist a teammate could verify without asking you questions. Not "it works" — "test X passes, output Y produced, PR merged, doc Z updated."_

### Demo
_At the end of the sprint, how will you show this working in 60 seconds? If you can't show it, rethink the scope._

### Estimate
_Rough. Hours, points, or T-shirt sizes — whatever the team uses. If it's bigger than a few days, break it up._

---

## Notes

- **Upstream/downstream matters most.** Most project failures aren't technical; they're interface mismatches. If two people don't know exactly what they're handing to each other, one of them is stuck.
- **Demo isn't just for the end.** A card that can be demoed is a card that produced something visible. If you can't demo it, it might be a task, not a story.
- **Subtasks live in the issue body**, not as separate issues, unless a subtask becomes a full day of work on its own.

# For team leads: adapting this template

This template is a starting point, not a script. How much you pre-fill vs. leave to the team is your call.

## The scaffolding dial

**High scaffolding** — fill in domain specifics, tighten acceptance criteria, hand the team a working backlog.
Use when: team is new to the domain, or the sprint is short, or you're early in the program and building confidence.

**Medium scaffolding** — fill in one or two examples per epic; leave the rest as prompts. Team drafts the rest in Sprint 0.
Use when: team has some experience and needs practice at decomposition without being lost.

**Low scaffolding** — hand over the epic titles and card template only. Team writes their own issues.
Use when: team is strong and the program's goal is planning practice as much as execution.

Most teams will benefit from starting one notch higher than you think and stepping down over sprints.

## What to watch for

- **Cards without a demo.** These become invisible work; the team can't tell if they're making progress.
- **Cards without a named upstream/downstream.** These become interface bugs three weeks later.
- **Cards that keep growing.** Split them. If a card has been "in progress" for a week, it was too big.
- **Silent team members.** If nobody's assigned to a card or nobody's talking about it, someone (probably you) needs to surface it.

## Rhythm suggestions (adapt freely)

- **Start of sprint:** team reviews upcoming epic(s), refines/splits issues together, assigns owners.
- **Daily:** brief written or verbal sync — what shipped, what's stuck, what's next.
- **End of sprint:** short demo of each epic's output. Team writes a 3-line retro: what worked, what didn't, one thing to try.

The point isn't Scrum ceremony. It's that these habits let the team catch problems before they compound.
